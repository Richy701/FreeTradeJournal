The most common reason trades never make it into a journal is not laziness. It is that the trader is on their phone and the platform has no export button. MT4 and MT5 on mobile, TradingView, a broker's own app, TopstepX on a tablet. You can see your closed trades. You cannot get them out.

So FreeTradeJournal reads screenshots.

## How it works

Take a screenshot of your closed trades or history. Any platform that shows symbol, direction, entry, exit and result will do. MT4 and MT5 on phone or desktop, TradingView, TopstepX, Tradovate, NinjaTrader, or your broker's app.

Open the Trade Log or the Dashboard, choose Import, and upload the image. You can add more than one screenshot at a time if your history runs over several screens.

The trades are read out into a table. Each row has the symbol, side, entry, exit, size, P&L and the open and close times. Nothing is saved yet.

![Screenshot import review](https://www.freetradejournal.com/screenshots/screenshot-import-review.png)

## Check the table before you import

This is the part I want you to actually do. Reading numbers off an image is not the same as reading a CSV, and the importer is honest about that.

- Rows it is less sure about are marked "Check this row". Look at those first.
- Trades you already have are unticked, so a second screenshot of the same screen does not create duplicates.
- Every cell is editable. If a decimal point was misread, fix it in the table.
- Only ticked rows are imported.

P&L is the figure shown on your platform. If commission and swap were visible in the screenshot they are subtracted, so what lands in the journal is what hit your account.

## What it costs

Free accounts get three screenshot imports. Pro accounts get 20 a day. If you have a CSV export available, use that instead. It is free without limit, exact to the cent, and it never misreads a digit.

## When to use it

Use screenshots for the trades that would otherwise be lost. A phone session on a Friday, a demo account on a platform that does not export, a firm dashboard that only shows history on screen. A journal with those trades in it is worth more than a tidy journal with gaps.
