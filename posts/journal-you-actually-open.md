I have watched a lot of people sign up for a journal, log trades for eight days, and never come back. I did the same thing with a spreadsheet twice before I built this. The problem is almost never motivation. It is that the routine was too long for the value it gave back, so it lost.

Here is the version that lasts. It takes under ten minutes a day and about twenty on a Sunday.

## Import, do not type

Typing trades in by hand is the first thing that kills the habit. Export from your broker and drop the file in, or take a screenshot if there is no export. The trades land with entry, exit, size and P&L filled. Your job is what the file cannot know: why you took it and how you felt.

## Write one entry per day, not per trade

A trade note is a sentence. A journal entry is the day. Open the journal after your session and write three things. What you did well, what you did badly, and what you will do differently tomorrow. Link the day's trades to the entry so the numbers sit next to the words.

![Journal entries](https://www.freetradejournal.com/images/screenshots/trading-journal-screenshot.png)

Tag the setup on each trade and pick an emotion. Two seconds each. In a month, the tag table and the emotion breakdown will tell you things you would swear were not true.

## Let the coach read it back

When you save a substantial entry, the coach reads it and replies with one observation about what you wrote and one question worth sitting with before the next session. No button to press, and you can turn it off. On days when you have written the same rule for the third time, the question tends to be about that.

## Sunday: twenty minutes

Once a week, open the calendar and Trade Insights. Look at four things and nothing else:

1. Which day of the week cost you money.
2. Which session made it.
3. The mistake tag with the biggest net cost.
4. Whether your average loser is bigger than your average winner.

Pick one thing from that list to change. Write it at the top of Monday's entry. That is the whole review.

## What kills it

- **Reviewing every trade.** You do not have time, so you stop. Review the day.
- **Journaling only red days.** The pattern you need is in the green ones too.
- **Waiting for a perfect system.** The tags you use now can be renamed later. Start.
